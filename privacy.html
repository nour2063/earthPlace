Privacy policy

Last updated 17 September 2026

earthPlace has no accounts. There is no sign-up, no password, and no email address. Your device is what the app recognises, and the display name you choose is only a label shown next to the blocks you place.

Who is responsible

earthPlace is made and run by Nour Elfangary, an individual developer, who is the data controller. Contact: noureldinesamehelfangary@gmail.com.

The app is offered to people in the United Kingdom and the European Union, so it is run to UK GDPR and EU GDPR. This policy applies to everyone who uses earthPlace, wherever they are.

What is collected, and why

Device identifier and key. When the app first reaches the server it registers an identifier for your install and receives a secret key. Everything the app sends is identified by that pair. On iPhone and iPad the identifier is a value the app generates for itself and keeps in the Keychain. On Android phones and on Quest it is an identifier Android gives the app, which is specific to this app and stays the same if you reinstall it. Either way it is not an advertising identifier, it is never shown to other users, and it is not shared with anyone. Without it there is no way to say which layers are yours, or to enforce a restriction. Lawful basis: performance of our agreement with you, and our legitimate interest in keeping the app usable and safe.

Display name. The name you choose is stored on your device and, so other people can see it, on the server. Anyone can pick any name, several devices can use the same one, and it identifies nobody by itself. Lawful basis: performance of our agreement with you.

Location, on the phone app. While you have the app open it uses your location to find layers near you, and the location of a layer you create is stored with it and shown publicly on the map. Location is also recorded with each block you place. The Quest app has no location features. Lawful basis: performance of our agreement with you for finding and placing layers, and our legitimate interest in understanding how the app is used for the block records.

Camera and your surroundings. The camera feed is processed on your device to place blocks in the world, and is not sent anywhere as video. Two things derived from it do leave your device for public layers: a spatial map of the place, generated by the Niantic Spatial SDK so that other people's devices can recognise the same spot; and still images from your camera, which become the layer's public picture. That includes screenshots you take on a public layer, and pictures the app takes by itself while you are building on one. Lawful basis: performance of our agreement with you.

What you build. Blocks (their position and colour), the author row they belong to, and layer names. Blocks on a public layer are visible to anyone who goes to that place and opens the layer there: they are anchored to the spot and cannot be viewed from anywhere else. Lawful basis: performance of our agreement with you.

Activity records. Each block placed or removed is recorded with the time, the layer, the position, the colour, your device identifier, and the location. Lawful basis: our legitimate interest in understanding how the app is used and in finding faults.

Reports, decisions, and appeals. If you report something: the reason, your comment, your device identifier, and a copy of the layer as it was at that moment. If a report is about you: the decision, any restriction, and anything you write in an appeal. Lawful basis: our legitimate interest in keeping the app safe for other users, and compliance with the app stores' requirements for apps with user content.

Security log. When a request looks like someone using an identity that isn't theirs, the server records what happened, the device identifier, and the IP address it came from. Lawful basis: our legitimate interest in security.

Private layers and blueprints stay on your device. A private layer's blocks pass through the multiplayer service to the people you invite, only while you are in it together, and are never stored on our server. Screenshots you save to your photo library stay on your device.

Is a device identifier personal data? Taken with the things above — your location, what you build, your IP address in the security log — it is treated as personal data under UK and EU GDPR, and this policy is written on that basis.

What other people can see

Only in that place, by someone standing there with the app open:

  • Your blocks, and the display name you placed them under.

Anywhere in the world, on the map:

  • The layers you created: their names, their locations, the picture taken from your camera, and the display name you created them under.

Never:

  • Your device identifier. It stays on the server.

Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → Delete my data. That removes this device's registration and key, its display name link, its device identifier from the activity records, and the link between the device and the author rows on layers. Your display name is cleared from the app, so nothing is re-linked unless you choose a name again.

Two things deliberately stay, and the app says so before you confirm:

  • Blocks and layers you have made. They belong to the places other people visit, so they stay as they are, with the names already shown next to them. The link back to your device is what goes.
  • Any restriction against your device, until it expires. Otherwise deleting your data would be a way around a ban.

Using earthPlace online afterwards registers the device again, because nothing online works without a registration.

You can also email noureldinesamehelfangary@gmail.com. Bear in mind there are no accounts: if you can't tell us which device install is yours, we may not be able to find your data, and neither UK nor EU data protection law requires us to keep extra information just to be able to identify you.

Who else processes it

These providers process data on our behalf, under contract:

  • DigitalOcean — hosting for the server.
  • MongoDB Atlas — the database.
  • Normal VR, Inc. (Normcore) — relays live block updates between people in the same layer.
  • OpenFreeMap — serves the map tiles your device requests, which exposes your IP address and the area you are looking at to them.

Niantic Spatial, Inc. provides the SDK that recognises places from your camera, and collects data through it for its own purposes, including location and data derived from your camera and sensors. It acts independently of us, under its own privacy policy.

Apple, Google, and Meta operate the stores the app is distributed through, under their own policies.

Nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising or tracking across other apps and sites.

Some of these providers are outside the UK, mainly in the United States. Where data is transferred there, it is done under the safeguards UK data protection law requires, such as the UK addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, ask us to restrict how it is used, object to use based on legitimate interests, or ask for it in a portable form. Email the address above and say which device install you mean.

If you are unhappy with how we handle it, you can complain to a regulator. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk; in the EU or EEA it is the data protection authority for the country you live in.

Children

earthPlace is not for children under 13. If we learn that a device belongs to someone under 13, its data is deleted.

Security

Traffic to the server uses HTTPS. The device key is held in storage only this app can read: the Keychain on iPhone and iPad, and the app's own private storage on Android phones and on Quest. Only a hash of the key is kept on the server, so it cannot be read back out of the database.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
